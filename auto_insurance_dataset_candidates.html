Dataset Candidates for an Auto Deductible vs Coinsurance Simulation

Six public insurance dataset candidates, their observation units and approximate or precise sizes, and a binary matrix showing whether each required or recommended simulation variable is available.

Web review date: 21 August 2026 6 dataset candidates 18 simulation-variable categories
Central data gap None of the six retained insurance datasets publishes a verified ground-up repair cost together with numeric deductible, contractual coinsurance, operative policy limit, claim expense or recovery data, and policyholder retention. A decision-grade simulation therefore requires combining datasets with actuarial assumptions or internal data.

Dataset profiles

“Observation” follows the source’s unit of analysis: policy row, claim row, customer-year, policy-year, vehicle configuration, sampled crash report, jurisdiction-year, or monthly index.

Core insurance microdata 12/18 variables

CAS Pricing Game 2016

Observation count / unit 87,228 policy rows + 4,568 claim rows

French private-motor material-damage data released for the 2016 pricing game. Separate policy and claim tables provide exposure, risk characteristics, claim charge, settlement year, deductible category, sum-insured category, and a compensation-rate field.

Principal limitation
Deductible and sum insured are categorical. The compensation rate is not verified as contractual policyholder coinsurance, and claim charge is not a verified ground-up repair cost.

Core insurance microdata 11/18 variables

Spanish Actual Motor Portfolio, 2015–2018

Observation count / unit 105,555 policy-period transactions; 30 variables

A real Spanish non-life motor portfolio covering November 2015 through December 2018. It contains policy dates, premiums, annual claim counts and costs, lapse or renewal information, and driver and vehicle risk characteristics.

Principal limitation
The main file aggregates claims by policy period. Coverage or claim-type detail is available only for a limited subset, and own-damage amounts may already be net of applicable deductibles.

Retention benchmark 6/18 variables

Guillén–Bolancé–Frees–Valdez Five-Year Data

Observation count / unit 40,284 customers; 122,935 customer-year rows

Longitudinal data for Spanish customers holding motor and homeowners contracts from 2010 to 2014. The file contains a persistent customer identifier, yearly renewal outcome, customer attributes, and annual motor and home claim counts and costs.

Principal limitation
Motor losses are annual aggregates, the portfolio combines motor and home relationships, and deductible, coinsurance, premium-change, and claim-level repair information are absent.

S4: Mendeley Data

Core insurance microdata 11/18 variables

Spanish Coverage-Specific Motor Portfolio, 2022–2024

Observation count / unit 354,140 policy-year records; 47 variables

An anonymised Spanish portfolio with coverage-level premiums, exposure, claim counts, and incurred amounts for property damage and other coverages. It also contains policy status, risk characteristics, and an indicator distinguishing comprehensive cover with versus without an excess.

Principal limitation
There is no numeric deductible, claim-level repair cost, contractual coinsurance field, or operative per-claim limit. Incurred amounts combine paid values and reserves.

Core insurance microdata 6/18 variables

freMTPL2

Observation count / unit 677,991 policy rows + 26,444 claim rows

A widely used French motor third-party-liability pair of frequency and severity files. The policy table includes exposure and rating characteristics; the severity table provides individual claim amounts linked by policy identifier.

Principal limitation
The coverage is third-party liability rather than own-damage collision, and the public files omit deductibles, limits, premiums, retention outcomes, and an explicit paid/incurred basis.

S7: CASdatasets documentation

Core insurance aggregates 7/18 variables

Brazilian brvehins1

Observation count / unit 1,965,355 policy/segment records

Brazilian vehicle-insurance data derived from the SUSEP AUTOSEG system for 2011. Records contain driver, vehicle, and area characteristics; exposure; premiums; average sum insured; and separate claim counts and amounts for partial and total collision and other guarantees.

Principal limitation
Losses are aggregated over the exposure period, there is no persistent policy identifier, and average sum insured is not necessarily the operative limit on an individual claim.

Qualification flags in the matrix

Q1–Q6 identify dataset-specific limitations. Q7 marks variables that are absent across all six retained candidates.

  1. Q1
    CAS contract and loss fields — The deductible and sum-insured fields are categories. The compensation-rate field is not confirmed as contractual policyholder coinsurance, and claim charge is not verified ground-up repair cost. Sources: S1
  2. Q2
    Spanish 2015–2018 aggregation — The principal file is organised by policy transaction and mainly reports policy-period claim counts and costs; more detailed claim information is limited. Own-damage values may already reflect policy terms. Sources: S2, S3
  3. Q3
    Five-year customer panel — Motor claim counts and costs are annual customer-level aggregates, only at-fault motor claims are included, and the same customers also hold homeowners contracts. Sources: S4
  4. Q4
    Spanish 2022–2024 field granularity — The excess field identifies cover with versus without an excess rather than a numeric amount. Policy status is not a causal renewal experiment, and financial amounts are coverage-level incurred values. Sources: S5, S6
  5. Q5
    freMTPL2 coverage and convention — The data cover third-party liability rather than own-damage collision. Some claim amounts may reflect French IRSA–IDA settlement conventions, and the public files do not state a full ground-up loss basis. Sources: S7
  6. Q6
    Brazilian aggregate fields — The sum-insured field is an average, not a verified operative per-claim limit. Claim counts and amounts are aggregated over the exposure period. Sources: S8
  7. Q7
    Cross-dataset ground-up and recovery gap — Across the candidates, verified ground-up loss and claim-expense/recovery fields are absent. No release alone identifies all sub-deductible events or the full amount above policy limits.
